# Bridging the Mobility Gap: A Study on Demand-Responsive Transit

**Course:** CE 694: Credit Seminar
**Programme:** M.Tech in Transportation Systems Engineering, IIT Bombay · May 2025

## Objective

To review the evolution of Demand-Responsive Transit (DRT) demand-modeling methods and to analyze how machine learning models can predict and explain DRT ridership demand.

## Brief Description

A systematic literature review of 14+ studies traced the evolution of DRT demand modeling from rule-based Dial-a-Ride Problem (DARP) methods to graph-convolutional deep learning. A published case study applying Random Forest, Bagging, ANN, and DNN models to predict daily trip production/distribution for an on-demand transit system was then analyzed in depth, including model interpretability via SHAP.

## Methodology

- Conducted a systematic literature review of 14+ studies spanning rule-based, statistical, and deep-learning DRT demand-modeling approaches.
- Analyzed a case study applying Random Forest, Bagging, ANN, and DNN models to predict daily trip production/distribution, achieving up to 72% classification accuracy.
- Applied SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis to interpret model outputs.
- Identified population density, income, and working-age population share as the dominant predictors of ridership demand.

## Tools Used

Python, Random Forest, Bagging, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Deep Neural Networks (DNN), SHAP (model interpretability).
